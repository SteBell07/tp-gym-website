1. What this document covers

This document applies to your use of all of the following at TP Fire & Ice:

  • Traditional sauna
  • Infrared sauna
  • Cold water immersion / ice baths
  • Contrast therapy (moving between heat and cold)
  • Compression boots

It explains the risks involved, what we ask of you, and the declaration you make when you accept these documents. For cold baths, you must also sign the separate Cold Bath Safety Notice in person before your first cold bath session.

2. Risks you need to know about

Heat, cold and compression therapies place real demands on the body. Even when they are used correctly and the Facility is well run, there are risks that cannot be removed entirely. These include:

Extreme heat and cold

Exposure to very high temperatures in the saunas, and very low water temperatures in the cold baths. Prolonged exposure can cause overheating, burns from hot surfaces, cold shock or a dangerous fall in body temperature.

Blood pressure changes

Heat widens blood vessels and cold narrows them. Moving between them can cause rapid changes in blood pressure and heart rate.

Dizziness and fainting

Particularly when standing up, leaving a sauna, or getting out of a cold bath.

Dehydration

Heavy sweating in the saunas causes fluid loss, which can lead to headaches, light-headedness and fatigue.

Strain on the heart

Heat and sudden cold both increase the work the heart and circulation must do. This can be serious for people with heart or circulatory conditions, including ones they don't know about.

Slips, trips and falls

Floors around saunas, showers and cold baths can be wet and slippery.

Using equipment wrongly

Staying in for too long, using equipment in ways you weren't shown, or adjusting settings yourself increases the risk of harm.

Adverse reactions

Including skin reactions, cold-induced reactions, breathlessness, numbness, nausea, discomfort or pain from compression, or a worsening of an existing condition.

Signing or accepting this declaration does not make taking part medically safe for you. It confirms that you understand the risks and will take part responsibly.

3. When to get medical advice first

If you have any doubt about whether these activities are suitable for you, please speak to your GP or another qualified medical professional before taking part. This is especially important if you:

  • have, or have had, any heart, circulatory or blood pressure condition;
  • are pregnant or think you may be pregnant;
  • have a history of fainting, seizures or epilepsy;
  • have diabetes, a respiratory condition, a skin condition, or have had recent surgery or a recent injury;
  • have, or have had, a blood clot (such as DVT), or have a condition affecting your legs or veins (relevant to compression boots);
  • take any medication that could affect your heart rate, blood pressure, temperature regulation, circulation or alertness; or
  • have any condition listed in the Cold Bath Safety Notice (Document 3). If so, you must not use the cold baths at all.

We are not able to give you medical advice, and our staff cannot tell you whether a medical condition makes it safe for you to take part.

4. Your responsibilities during every session

  • Stop straight away and tell a member of staff if you feel dizzy, faint, sick, confused, short of breath, have chest pain or palpitations, notice numbness or unusual skin changes, or feel unwell in any way.
  • Tell staff before your session about any change in your health or medication since your last visit.

Cold Bath Safety Notice

Read

Important: read before your first cold bath session

This notice must be handed to you, signed and dated by you before your first cold bath session. We keep a copy for inspection by our insurer. Ticking the box at online checkout does not replace signing this notice.

A. You must not use cold bath therapy if you suffer from any of the following:

  1. a) Abnormal / altered skin sensation
  2. b) Impaired circulation: tissue damage may result from vasoconstriction
  3. c) Open wounds / broken skin
  4. e) Hypersensitivity to cold, such as Raynaud's phenomenon, cold urticaria, cryoglobulinemia, and paroxysmal cold haemoglobinuria
  5. f) Angina pectoris or other severe cardiac disease
  6. g) Chlorine allergy
  7. h) Peripheral vascular disease
  8. i) Under influence of drugs or alcohol
  9. j) Epilepsy

If you are unsure whether any of these apply to you, do not use the cold baths. Get advice from your GP first.

Review note (insurer): This list is copied word for word from the insurer's schedule, including its lettering. The schedule as supplied goes from c) to e) with no item d). Ask NBS Underwriting whether an item is missing and, if so, what it says, before this notice is used.

B. Rules for every cold bath session

  • Cold bath therapy is only available to people aged 16 and over.
  • A cold bath must never be used by any one person whilst alone at the premises. Cold baths are only available when a trained member of staff is on site.
  • Do not use a cold bath until a trained member of staff has checked the water temperature for that session.
  • Use the cold bath only in the way you have been shown, and follow all staff instructions on entry, exit and time in the water.
  • Get in and out slowly using the steps or handholds provided. Floors may be wet.
  • Get out straight away and tell staff if you feel faint, dizzy, short of breath, have chest pain, uncontrollable shivering, numbness or any unusual skin reaction.
  • Tell staff before each session if your health has changed, including any of the conditions in part A.

C. Our cold bath safety standards

As a condition of our insurance, we make sure that:

  1. Any instructor or therapist is appropriately qualified or has at least 2 years' experience.
  2. The equipment is installed, maintained and used in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions and guidelines and is inspected, and water temperatures are checked before use, by a responsible and fully trained employee, and any defects are remedied.
  3. The cold baths are kept clean and adequately chlorinated at all times.
  4. The area around the cold baths is checked regularly for tripping and slipping hazards.
  5. All users receive appropriate instruction in the use of the equipment, and records of this instruction are kept in an accessible and clear form.
  6. Cold bath therapy is only made available to people aged 16 and over.
  7. A cold bath is not used by any one person whilst alone at the premises.
  8. This notice is handed to, and signed and dated by, each user before their first session, a copy is kept for inspection, and it is prominently displayed in the reception area and by the cold baths.
